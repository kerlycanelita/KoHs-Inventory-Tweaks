# KoHs Inventory Tweaks 1.2.1

**Update if you play on servers with Super Fast Inventory on.** Every earlier
version sends what you do in an inventory it opened early before the server has
been told you stopped moving. Anti-cheats check for exactly that. On an older
version, turn Super Fast Inventory off on servers.

## The fix

### What was wrong

Vanilla opens the inventory inside a client tick. That tick releases the keys
and tells the server so: the movement keys are up, the sprint has ended, the
head has made its last turn. Only an input poll after that tick can click a
slot. A server therefore never receives a container click from a player it
still believes to be sprinting forward.

Super Fast Inventory builds the screen between two ticks, up to 50 ms sooner.
Up to 1.2.0, a shortcut or a click made before that tick ran was handled at
once and its packet left ahead of the tick's: a container click from a
sprinting player, followed by the stop and a turn of the head. No Vanilla
client can send that order, and servers that check inventories look for it.

Earlier notes and the wiki said a server could not tell an early opening
apart. That was wrong, and they are corrected.

### What 1.2.1 does

Only the screen is early. Keys, clicks, the wheel and typed characters that
arrive in an inventory opened early are kept until its tick has run:

- The first of them is handled at the first input poll after that tick, the
  earliest moment a Vanilla client could act in that inventory. Each one after
  it follows as far behind it as you made it.
- Whatever you do within a tick of something that was handled late is kept the
  same way, so two actions never reach the server closer together than your
  hand made them, unless a full tick still separates them.
- Each one acts where the pointer was, on the slot that was highlighted, when
  you made it. The pointer itself is never held.
- Once one of them closes the inventory, the rest was made in the world and
  goes there at once.
- If nothing arrived before the tick ran, nothing is kept and nothing changes.

They go through Minecraft's own handlers. Nothing is generated, repeated or
reordered, and nothing is ever handled sooner than you did it.

So pressing the inventory key and the offhand key in one roll still works, and
it lands at the first moment it legally can: what you gain is the screen up to
50 ms sooner and a shortcut that is never wasted on the world.

## Verification

The new `inventory-order` lab records every packet the client sends around an
opening and judges them with the inventory rules of TotemGuard, an open-source
anti-cheat for crystal PvP servers: a click or a close while sprinting or
moving; movement, sprint, aim or a world action with the inventory open; a
click after a close in one tick. Each trial sprints forward turning the camera,
presses the inventory key at a random moment of the tick, flicks to a totem and
presses the offhand key 0 to 42 ms later.

| | Fast re-totems flagged | Vanilla re-totems flagged |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1.2.0, Minecraft 26.2 | 23 of 48 | 0 of 20 |
| 1.2.1, all five versions | 0 of 48 on each | 0 of 20 on each |

On 1.2.0 it flagged 8 of 8 with the offhand key in the same frame as the
opening, 6 of 8 at 6 ms, 5 of 8 at 12 ms, 2 of 8 at 20 and at 30 ms, and none
at 42 ms. On 1.2.1 every totem still reaches the offhand.

It also presses a second shortcut 8 to 90 ms after the first, 20 times, and
checks that the two clicks reach the server no closer together than they were
made, or a tick apart; and it closes the inventory and presses a hotbar key
8 ms later, 6 times, and checks that the inventory closes and the held slot
changes, in that order.

Run in the game on 26.2, 26.1.2, 26.1.1, 26.1 and 1.21.11, together with the
`slot-shortcuts`, `pvp-input`, `features`, `close-hotbar` and `cursor-contract`
labs.
